### Hi, I'm Ana 👋

I came to AI from psychology, and I keep asking the same question I learned there.
How sure can we be that a measurement means what it says?
Today I ask it about AI agents, benchmarks and the numbers people quote about them.

**What I've made so far**

**[Agent Reliability Calculator](https://github.com/anaexplores/agent-reliability-calculator)** shows why adding more verifiers stops helping after a point and why retrying an agent buys less than the usual formula promises. The defaults come from 131,332 real agent runs on four public benchmarks. You can [try it in the browser](https://anaexplores.github.io/agent-reliability-calculator/).

**[Honest agent scores](https://github.com/anaexplores/honest-agent-scores)** asks whether broken benchmark tasks or repeated runs change which AI agent comes out on top. On 3,300 Terminal-Bench 4.0 runs the answer was mostly no. The surprise was that broken tasks can make agents look different in reliability when they are not.

**[The Signature Explorer](https://github.com/anaexplores/the-signature-explorer)** explains digital signatures from the one in your crypto wallet to the post-quantum ones. You can sign a message, break it and even forge a signature yourself, and every fact on the page links to its original source. [Open the site](https://anaexplores.github.io/the-signature-explorer/).

**How I work**

I write down the analysis plan before I run the numbers. If the result is negative, I publish it anyway. Every figure in my projects can be traced back to where it came from.
